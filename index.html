Subscription & Credits

Your institution operates on a subscription model that controls how many students you can register, how many enrollments you can create, and how many CS/CBC AI generations you can run — both per day and in total for the subscription period.

How Subscriptions Work

Student Slots

Each plan has a max_students limit. Every new student account created (by staff or via API) consumes one slot. If all slots are used, no new students can be registered until the plan is renewed or a new plan is added.

Students Used 234 / 500

Enrollment Slots

Each plan has a max_enrollments limit. Each time a student is enrolled in a course instance, one slot is consumed. A single student taking 3 courses uses 3 enrollment slots.

Enrollments Used 876 / 2000

AI Generation Limits (CS & CBC generators only)

CS & CBC AI generation is governed by two caps set per plan, plus one rule:

  • Daily limit (max_ai_generations_per_day) — a per-day allowance that resets at midnight Philippine time (UTC+8). With stacked plans, your effective daily limit is the highest value among active plans; if none is set, the platform default of 100/day applies.
  • Total (period) limit (max_ai_generations_total) — a hard cap for the whole subscription duration that does not reset. With stacked plans your total budget is the sum of active plans' totals (each plan tracks its own consumption); if a plan leaves it blank, the platform default of 100 applies for that plan.
  • No active subscription = no AI. If your institution has no active, in-date plan, CS/CBC AI generation is blocked with a "needs more credits" message until a plan is activated or renewed.

Each successful generation counts one against both your daily and period totals (internal retries don't count). Course, module, and exam AI generation are separate and not counted here.

Stackable Plans

Multiple plans can be active simultaneously. When one fills up, usage spills to the next available plan automatically.

Time-Bound

Each plan has a start and expiry date. Expired plans stop accepting new usage, but existing students/enrollments remain active.

Unlimited & Defaults

A value of 0 for max_students or max_enrollments means unlimited for that metric. The AI limits work differently: if a plan leaves them blank, the platform default (100) applies. Stacked plans use the highest value for the daily limit and the sum of values for the total (period) limit.

Visible in the Staff Console

The Staff Console surfaces these credits live: student & enrollment usage bars (stacked across plans), a burn rate indicator (how fast enrollment slots are consumed and how many days remain at the current pace), and an "AI Generation Quota (CS & CBC generators only)" panel with two bars — today's usage vs. your daily limit, and this period's usage vs. your total budget. If your institution has no active subscription, the panel shows a red "AI generation disabled — needs more credits" state instead. This helps you plan renewals and manage AI usage before running out.

CS Generator

The Competency Standards (CS) Generator lets you build a complete, TESDA-formatted Competency Standards document — qualifications, their competency units, and full training requirements — and export it as a polished Word (.docx) file. AI assists every step, but you stay in control: nothing is final until you review and save.

Where to find it & what you'll see

Open it from the sidebar menu item "CS Generator". You manage your own institution's qualifications here. The shared TESDA-standard building blocks (Basic competencies and global Common competencies) appear as read-only — they're maintained centrally so every institution uses the same national standard, while anything your institution creates is fully editable.

The Big Picture — How a CS Document Is Built

A Competency Standard is made of layers. Understanding them makes everything else obvious:

Qualification

The program itself (e.g., "Automotive Servicing NC I"). Has a title, sector, PQF level, description, and job titles.

Units of Competency

Grouped into Basic, Common, and Core. Each unit is a distinct skill area.

Elements & Criteria

Inside each unit: Elements (sub-skills), each with Performance Criteria, Required Knowledge, and Required Skills.

Range of Variables & Evidence Guide

Per unit: the conditions/tools it applies to, and how competence is assessed.

Section 3 — Training Requirements

Trainee entry requirements, trainer qualifications, tools/equipment/materials, and training facilities.

Acknowledgements

The people and organizations credited in the final document.

Basic, Common & Core Competencies

TESDA Basic

TESDA-standard, shared to everyone, organized by PQF level (e.g., "Participate in workplace communication"). Staff see them read-only. Auto-assigned to your qualification by PQF level.

Common (per sector)

Shared per sector. There are two kinds: TESDA (global) ones — read-only for staff — and My Institution ones your institution creates and can fully edit. Auto-assigned by sector.

Core (this qualification)

The technical skills unique to this qualification. You build these in the Core Competencies tab. Each core unit belongs to the qualification you're editing.

How to tell them apart on screen: In the Common Competencies manager, each unit shows a badge — a purple TESDA badge (global, read-only for staff) or a green My Institution badge (yours, editable). If a unit is read-only for you, its edit/delete controls are hidden and a lock banner explains why.

Export to Word (.docx)

Open a qualification and click "Download .docx" to get the full, formatted document: cover page, the legal page (the RA 7796 "Technical Education and Skills Development Act of 1994" — which always starts on page 2), purpose, table of contents, Section 1 & 2 (all units), Section 3, and Acknowledgements.

AI Generation Quota

Every AI Generate action in the CS and CBC generators counts against your institution's AI quota (course/module/exam AI is separate and not counted here). Each successful generation counts one against both a daily limit (resets midnight Philippine time, UTC+8) and a total period limit (spans the whole subscription duration, does not reset); internal retries don't count. If you hit either cap — or if your institution has no active subscription — you'll see a clear message ("needs more credits"). Your remaining daily and period quotas appear in the "AI Generation Quota (CS & CBC generators only)" panel on the Staff Console. Limits can be raised per institution via its subscription plan.

CBC Generator

The Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) Generator turns a finished Competency Standard into a training curriculum — the document trainers actually deliver from — and exports it as a formatted Word file. It is a derived document: it reads your CS content live and reshapes it into curriculum form, so you never re-encode competencies.

CS → CBC: what maps to what

A CBC is built from a CS Qualification. The generator transforms it: Qualification → Course · Unit of Competency → Module of Instruction (title converted to -ing "gerund" form) · Element → Learning Outcome · Performance Criteria → Assessment Criteria · Knowledge + Skills → Contents · Range of Variables → Conditions · Evidence Guide methods → Assessment Methods. You work only with your own institution's qualifications and curricula.

The "gerund" module titles: A unit named "Receive and respond to workplace communication" becomes the module "Receiving and Responding to Workplace Communication". The generator does this automatically; a Gerund Review tab lets you check and override any title that doesn't read well.

Creating a CBC — Step by Step

Click "New CBC" and pick an eligible CS Qualification. The list shows how many competency units each qualification has and how many CBCs already exist for it.
Course Design tab — review the auto-computed Basic/Common/Core hours and total; fill Course Title, Description, Delivery, and Assessment Methods. Set Elective Hours and SIL Hours here if applicable (see note below).
Modules & Learning Outcomes tab — each module (gerundized unit) lists its Learning Outcomes. For each LO you can click AI Draft to generate Conditions and Methodologies (the two fields with no direct CS source), review, and save.
Resources tab — the tools/equipment come from the CS; you add the two curriculum-only areas here (see below), each with its own AI Generate.
Gerund Review tab — check the auto-generated module titles; override any that read oddly.
Download .docx — export the full curriculum document.

Resources — Contextual & Learning Resource Areas

The Resources tab has two curriculum-only lists that each have their own purple "AI Generate" button:

Contextual Learning Area AI Generate

Spaces for theory/classroom learning (lecture/discussion area, audio-visual area, simulation area). AI suggests rows from the course's modules, tools/equipment, and facilities.

Learning Resource Area AI Generate

Where learning references live (library/reference area, learning modules/CBLMs, manuals, e-learning access). AI suggests relevant rows for this specific qualification.

Click AI Generate for either area and it appends suggested rows (quantity, unit, description) for you to review — then click "Save Resource Areas" to keep them. Like all CS/CBC AI, this counts against your daily quota.

What are Elective Hours & SIL Hours? Elective Hours = time for optional/specialization (elective) competencies. SIL Hours = Supervised Industry Learning — the workplace/on-the-job training portion. These are manual fields that default to N/A because there's no competency data to compute them from — type your values in the Course Design tab and save. Basic/Common/Core hours, by contrast, are computed automatically from the units.

Your CS is never touched

The CBC reads the CS live and stores only CBC-specific extras (course fields, per-outcome Conditions/Methodologies overrides, resource areas, module-hour/title overrides). Editing or deleting a CBC never changes the underlying Competency Standard. If you update the CS later, the CBC reflects the change automatically. Note that a competency unit disabled on the CS qualification is excluded from the CBC and its hour totals.

API Integration Overview

All external API endpoints live under /api/v1/external/ and require the X-API-Key header.

Example: Create a Student Account

curl -X POST https://your-lms.com/api/v1/external/accounts \
  -H "X-API-Key: tldsk_your_api_key_here" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "email": "student@example.com",
    "full_name": "Juan Dela Cruz",
    "password": "SecurePass123!",
    "uli": "ULI-2026-0001234"
  }'

Authentication & Security

API keys are institution-scoped — you can only access data within your own institution.

Key Format

tldsk_ + 48 hex characters (54 total)

Sent via X-API-Key header on every request.

Institution Scoping

Every query is filtered to your institution. There is no way to access another institution's students, courses, or enrollments.

Scopes

Keys have specific permissions (e.g., accounts:read, enrollments:write). The staff who generates the key selects which scopes to grant — individually, or via a preset bundle: read_only (all read scopes), enrollment_manager (accounts + enrollments read/write, plus courses/instances read), or full_access (all scopes).

Quota Enforcement

API operations respect your subscription limits. Creating a student or enrollment via API consumes slots just like the web UI.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when our subscription runs out of student slots?
No new student accounts can be created until your plan is renewed or a new plan is added by the platform administrator. Existing students and enrollments remain fully functional.
Can a student be enrolled in multiple courses?
Yes. Each enrollment in a different course instance consumes one enrollment slot. The same student can take as many courses as your plan allows.
Can teachers create or edit course content?
Teachers have read-only access to course content. Course creation and content editing is done by the platform admin or content creators (with admin/developer role).
Is there a mobile app?
The platform is a responsive web application that works on any modern mobile browser. No native app installation required — simply open the URL in Chrome, Safari, or Firefox on your phone.
How does the API quota work with our subscription?
API operations follow the exact same subscription limits as the web interface. Creating a student via API consumes one student slot, creating an enrollment consumes one enrollment slot. The oldest active plan is consumed first (spillover to next plan when full).
What happens if a student fails all assessment attempts?
The student is flagged as "at risk" in the teacher's student tracker. Staff or teachers can then decide to reset attempts (by admin intervention) or provide additional support. The enrollment remains active.
Why can't I edit some competency units in the CS Generator?
Units with a purple TESDA badge are TESDA-standard (global) and are maintained centrally — they're read-only for staff. Basic competencies and global Common competencies fall in this category. To have your own editable version, create a Common competency under your institution (it shows a green "My Institution" badge and is fully editable).
My AI generation stopped with a "limit reached / needs more credits" message — what now?
The CS & CBC generators are governed by two per-institution caps: a daily quota (resets at midnight Philippine time, UTC+8) and a total period quota (spans the whole subscription duration and does not reset). You'll also be blocked entirely if your institution has no active subscription. Check the Staff Console's "AI Generation Quota (CS & CBC generators only)" panel to see which limit you've hit. If it's the daily cap, wait for the reset; if it's the period cap or a missing subscription, ask the platform administrator to renew/add a plan or raise your institution's limits via its subscription plan. (Course/module/exam AI generation is separate and not affected by these quotas.)
I built core units but they disappeared — why?
When creating a brand-new qualification, core units are held in the editor until you click the big "Create Qualification" button — closing the modal first discards them. When editing an existing qualification, "Save Unit Changes" saves each core unit to the database immediately, so it won't be lost.
Does editing a CBC change the original Competency Standard?
No. The CBC reads the CS live and stores only curriculum-specific extras and overrides. Editing or deleting a CBC never alters the underlying CS. If you later update the CS, the CBC reflects those changes automatically.
How do I get support?
Contact your institution's staff or the platform administrator. For technical/API issues, developers can reference the interactive documentation at /developer.
